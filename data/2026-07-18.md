vLLM quietly introduced something local LLM enthusiasts have been waiting for for a long time: any small model can now perform speculative decoding for any large model.  A different tokenizer, a different model family - it doesn't matter anymore. Previously, you needed a shared tokenizer or a pre-trained draft head, which most models lack.  The small model guesses several tokens ahead, and the large one verifies them in a single pass; everything comes down to the acceptance rate, the percentage of correctly guessed tokens.  There were no public benchmarks, so I ran the first ones:  Mistral base: +37% (65% acceptance) Mistral Instruct: +8% (46% acceptance) Gemma 4: -26% (32% acceptance) Qwen 3.5/3.6: don't work yet  The results are mediocre: on template-based synthetic tasks, the same pair previously yielded +88% with 99% acceptance, but on real-world questions, the acceptance rate drops by half, rendering it pointless.  As of today, the feature is a week old; the mechanism works in principle (the output is bit-for-bit identical to no-spec), but we need an acceptance rate above ~50% to make it usable. As soon as they improve the draft matching, this will be free speed for all models without dedicated heads.  I'm keeping an eye on @vllm_project
