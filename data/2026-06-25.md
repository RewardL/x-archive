### 00:17 @unknown

What is actually limiting model routers for coding tasks?  Most routers treat picking a model as a static, one-off classification.  This paper identifies the real bottleneck as information deficit. Simply augmenting a vanilla LLM router with task-dimension-level performance statistics yields a 15.3% relative gain, surpassing a heuristic router built on the same priors.  Motivated by that, the authors propose Agent-as-a-Router, which formalizes routing as a Context to Action to Feedback to Context loop. It closes the information gap by accumulating execution-grounded experience during deployment, so the router improves from real outcomes instead of guessing once.  Why does it matter?  If you orchestrate multiple coding models by cost and skill, treating routing as a learning loop rather than a one-shot classifier is the lever. This means that routing becomes an agentic process that updates its priors as it runs.  Paper: https://t.co/717q9Ep45M  Learn to build effective AI agents in our academy: https://t.co/LRnpZN7L4c

### 02:30 @unknown

AI has its own language. And nobody taught you how to speak it.  You've nodded through "LLMs", "RAG", and "RLHF" You've googled them at 11pm after a meeting.  That ends today.  Here's what every term you've been pretending to know actually means:  1. LLM (Large Language Model) The AI brain behind ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot. Trained on billions of documents. Predicts the most likely next word — at scale.  2. ML (Machine Learning) Computers that learn from examples — not hardcoded rules. Feed it data. It finds the patterns itself. The foundation everything else here is built on.  3. Hallucination When AI makes stuff up. Confidently. Completely wrong. Happens when training data has gaps.  4. Token The building block of AI communication. Not a word — a chunk of a word. More tokens = more cost in enterprise settings.  5. Context Window How much the model can "see" at once. Its short-term memory. Run out of room — and it forgets where you started.  6. AGI (Artificial General Intelligence) AI that can learn any task a human can. Not built for one job — adaptable to all. Still theoretical. The finish line everyone's racing toward.  7. MCP (Model Context Protocol) A universal connector for AI. Links models to your apps, files, and tools. Think USB-C — but for AI.  8. Training vs. Inference Training = teaching the model. Inference = the model doing the work. You can't have the second without the first.  9. Fine-tuning Taking a general model. Feeding it specialized data. Making it smarter at one specific thing.  10. Reinforcement Learning (RLHF) RLHF = Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback AI learns by trial, reward, and repeat. Like training a dog — but the dog is a neural network.  11. Distillation Teaching a small model to mimic a large one. Faster. Cheaper. Nearly as good. Likely how GPT-4 Turbo was built.  12. RAG RAG = Retrieval-Augmented Generation. AI + your own documents. Reduces hallucinations dramatically.  13. Chain of Thought Breaking a complex problem into steps. Like showing your work in math class. Slower — but far more accurate.  14. Weights Numbers that shape what the model "knows." Start random. Adjust during training. The model's actual learned intelligence — stored as math.  15. Validation Loss A score that tells you how well training is going. Lower = better. Helps catch overfitting before it's too late.  The people who understand this language aren't just more informed.  They ask better questions. They make better decisions. They sound like they belong in the room.
